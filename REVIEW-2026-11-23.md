# Review record, 23 November 2026

This is what happened when I ran the docket in this repository over the repository itself, in the hours before I first published it.

I asked for the review and chose the method. Claude agents ran it: one orchestrator, worker agents in separate git worktrees, wardens that audit briefs and merges, and verifiers that had not seen the work being produced. No finding was sent up to me for a ruling.

## Why it is here

The problem I most want to understand is the fix that passes every test and leaves the fault in place. This is a small pilot on that problem, on my own code, with the numbers as they came out.

## What was measured

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Self-claim sweep by one fresh reviewer | 90 claims the package made about itself: 76 confirmed, 10 refuted, 4 could not be tested |
| Round 1 of fixes, every test passing (45 and 28 cases) | A fresh verifier attacked 12 fixes: 8 closed, 4 not closed |
| Round 2 of fixes, every test passing (54 and 42 cases) | 6 fixes attacked: 5 closed, 1 not closed, and 1 new silent failure found |
| Round 3 of fixes, every test passing (55 and 53 cases) | 5 fixes attacked: 5 closed, nothing serious found |

Across the three rounds, 5 of 23 fixes were not closed when a reviewer that had not seen the work attacked them, although every test passed each time. None was judged to be a fix that hides the symptom and leaves the defect.

## The other checks

- The tests were written before the fixes. Each round, the new test cases were run against the unfixed code first. They failed on the findings and on nothing else: 13 of 45 and 8 of 28 before round 1, 8 of 54 and 9 of 42 before round 2, 1 of 55 and 7 of 53 before round 3.
- A warden audited the task briefs before dispatch, twice. Both audits changed the briefs before any worker started.
- A warden checked each branch before merge and held 3 of the 6 hive branches. All three holds were real: a crash that could still be reached, a line that still implied the hooks use a flag they do not use, and a false sentence left in place.
- The ownership gate is a script, not an agent. It passed every branch, and it was shown to reject a branch that edited a file outside its task.

## What each kind of check caught

- The tests caught what they were written for, and regressions. Nothing else.
- The wardens caught departures from the brief.
- The verifiers caught faults in the merged whole: drift in files with accented names, staged deletions passing the hook, and a silent pass when the project sits in a subfolder of a bigger repository.

## The count

60 findings went in. 46 were closed with evidence. 12 were rejected with a written argument. 2 were left open at the time: the repository had no licence file, and some things were not tested (the Stop hook in a live Claude Code session, the GitHub Actions workflow, Windows).

## Limits of this record

- One night, one repository, 23 fixes. It is a pilot, not a study.
- The builders, wardens and verifiers were all Claude models. Independence here means a separate context, not a different model.
- The orchestrating agent wrote both the task briefs and the tests.
- "Not closed" is a verifier's judgement against an acceptance criterion written before the fix.
- I did not check the fixes line by line. How much of that checking a person can safely hand over is the question I want to study.
